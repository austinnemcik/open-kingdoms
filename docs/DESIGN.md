# Open Kingdoms — design

A free, open-source kingdom strategy game: build a city, gather on a shared
world map, raise armies led by historical commanders, and fight alongside an
alliance for control of the kingdom. Mechanically close to Rise of Kingdoms,
with no monetisation and all original assets.

## Pillars

1. **Fair.** Nothing can be bought. Progress comes from playing and cooperating.
2. **Respect players' time.** Timers are measured in minutes and hours, not
   weeks. Seasons let new players catch up.
3. **Alliance-first.** The best moments are coordinated: rallies, territory,
   forts, holy sites.
4. **Open.** Anyone can host a kingdom, read the rules, or mod the data files.

## Core loop

City (build, research, train) → World map (gather, hunt barbarians, scout,
fight) → Alliance (territory, rallies, objectives) → Season events (kingdom vs
kingdom) → back to the city with rewards.

## Systems

- **Resources:** food, wood, stone (City Hall 4+), gold (City Hall 10+).
  Produced by city buildings (lazily computed: `stored + rate * elapsed`, capped)
  and gathered from map nodes.
- **Buildings:** gated by City Hall level. Upgrades take time and resources.
  Two builder slots (both free, no paid second builder).
- **Troops:** infantry, cavalry, archers, siege; tiers T1–T5. Counter triangle:
  infantry > cavalry > archers > infantry. Siege is strong against structures.
- **Hospital:** wounded troops heal; overflow dies. Severe wounds vs deaths in
  PvP keep fights meaningful without total loss.
- **Commanders:** historical figures with original skills. One primary and one
  secondary per army, three talent trees, four skills, levels and stars.
  Acquired and upgraded through play (events, PvE, alliance shop), never gacha.
- **World map:** large tile grid per kingdom, chunked for interest management.
  Resource nodes, barbarians, barbarian forts, passes, holy sites, alliance
  flags and forts. Marches move in real time; armies can be intercepted.
- **Combat:** deterministic round-based simulation (about 1 round per second).
  Damage from attack vs defence, troop counts, counters and commander buffs.
  Rage builds each round; the active skill fires at full rage.
- **Alliances:** chat, help (cut a timer by a small fixed amount), alliance
  research, gifts from PvE, territory, rallies, garrisons.
- **Seasons:** linked kingdoms compete in a shared zone, then reset or merge.

## Economy without microtransactions

Commercial games of this type pace progression around purchases. We remove the
store and rebalance:

- Target roughly 3–4 months of regular play to reach max City Hall.
- Speedups exist only as earned rewards and are modest; time is the main gate.
- No premium currency. If a "gems" style currency exists, it is earned only and
  spent on cosmetics or convenience that cannot be converted into power.
- VIP is replaced by an account level earned through daily activity.
- Hosting is paid for by donations or by the community running servers.
  Donations never grant in-game advantages.
- Anti-bot: server-side rate limits and anomaly detection, since botting
  replaces paying as the main way to gain an unfair edge.

## Art direction

3D, high-angle isometric camera, stylised low-poly. Readable silhouettes,
saturated but not neon colours, clear team colours on the map. All models are
generated by Python scripts in Blender (`tools/blender/`) so agents can make
and iterate on them. Buildings visibly grow every few levels (tiers at
levels 1, 6, 11, 16, 21).

## Working title

"Open Kingdoms". It's a placeholder and easy to rename (`client/project.godot`,
README, login screen).

## Game time

All server/core timestamps are unsigned Unix seconds. Data durations use seconds;
resource rates use units per hour. The server injects a Clock (wall clock in
production, manual clock in tests) and calls pure rules with explicit `now`.
A one-second tick processes timers; lazy access handles offline progress. Clock
corrections must not reverse accounted time or grant the same production twice.

## City production

Production and storage contributions use per-level growth curves in buildings.yaml.
At level 1, farms/lumber mills produce 600/hour, quarries 300/hour and goldmines
120/hour. City Hall starts with 2,000 capacity per resource, each producer adds
500 for its resource, and a storehouse adds 5,000 per resource. Early farm builds
are 45 seconds, upgrades 60 seconds, and City Hall upgrades begin at two minutes.
Integer accounting preserves fractions across collections; offline accrual caps
at storage. Production never removes existing over-cap resources. Snapshots carry
resources, rates_per_hour, capacity and as_of (Unix seconds) for client interpolation.
